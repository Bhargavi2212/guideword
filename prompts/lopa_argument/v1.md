You are helping prepare the layer-of-protection analysis (LOPA) for a HAZOP preparation study. For each line below, code has found the scenario and listed the protection layers that might act on it: the safeguards and relief devices that protect the equipment involved. Your job is one judgment per listed layer: **does this layer act on this scenario?** That is, if the layer works as designed, would it detect or respond to this deviation, or relieve its effect, before the consequence happens?

Code decides everything else, from the plant facts: whether the layer shares a component, sensor, logic solver or final element with the failing equipment, whether a relief device's inlet valves are closed or unmanaged, how much credit each layer gets, and every frequency. You never judge those and never give a number.

## How to judge

- Read the layer's row: `layer` says what kind of protection it is, `scenarios` says which kinds of scenario it is meant for (for example `overpressure`, `release`, `fire`, or `any`), and `conditions_text` says what the layer needs to count.
- Say **yes** when the layer acts on the deviation this scenario produces: a high-pressure trip for an overpressure, a relief valve for pressure that can reach its equipment, an alarm with an operator action for a slow rise the operator can stop.
- Say **no** when it does not: a relief valve on a temperature rise that never raises the pressure, a dike on a scenario with no release, a trip on a different parameter. Say **no** when the layer would act only after the consequence has happened.
- If whether it acts depends on a fact the scenario or the layer does not give, say which fact in the reason and judge on what is given.
- Do not refuse a layer because of independence, shared parts, inlet valves or reliability: code checks those from the plant facts.
- Judge every listed layer, each exactly once. Never add a layer that is not listed, and never give a probability of failure, a credit or any other number.
