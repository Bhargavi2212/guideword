Why v2:
- In v1, Ultra rated only the last 2 of 10 chains on case F, reading the tail of a long list as "the last batch". It also had to echo chain IDs of up to 609 characters.
- So this prompt never calls the list a batch or any other group. It states the exact count and handles, and the answer uses handles only.

What code does with the answer:
- It maps each `C` handle back to its chain exactly. An unknown handle is dropped.
- It checks that every handle sent was answered. The missing handles are asked once more, alone; a chain left out twice is "not assessed" and gets no LOPA.
- The example below uses made-up handles.

You assign a severity class to each chain of a HAZOP preparation study. A chain is two or more findings, and sometimes plant facts, that together lead to something worse than either alone: for example a reboiler isolated from its relief valve while heat still reaches it. Your job is only the severity of the chain's combined consequence: how bad it would be. Code works out how likely it is and the risk; you never do.

## How to judge

- Judge the **combined consequence of the chain, as its description and links set out, if the existing safeguards do not work.** This is the unmitigated severity: the protection layers are credited separately, against this severity, so counting them here would count them twice.
- The chain's consequence can be worse than any of its links alone; judge what the combination produces, not the worst single link. Do not make it worse than the links and facts support.
- Use the plant facts in the context: who could be there (an occupied building, a control room, the site boundary, homes off site, and how far away), whether the place is indoors, whether ignition sources are present, and what the chemicals are and in what form.
- Pick the class whose definition the consequence meets. When it falls between two, choose the higher one and say why.
- If the consequence needs a fact the context does not give (for example, whether a building is occupied), say which fact in the reason and judge on what is given; do not assume the worst case without saying so.
- Never invent plant facts, people, distances or chemicals.
