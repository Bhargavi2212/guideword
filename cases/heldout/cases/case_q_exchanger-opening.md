# Plant Q: hydrogen recovery section of a polycrystalline silicon plant — exchanger removal and cleaning

## Data package

**Process / products**
- Coastal chemical site (in a designated industrial disaster-prevention district) producing high-purity polycrystalline silicon (semiconductor grade, about 2,800 t/yr across two plants), silicon tetrachloride (STC), trichlorosilane (TCS) and dichlorosilane (DCS).
- Process (trichlorosilane route):
  1. Chlorination: metallurgical silicon powder (about 98% Si) reacts with HCl in a fluidised-bed furnace at about 300 °C to give mainly TCS plus H2 (some STC).
  2. Distillation: multi-stage columns purify TCS, including chlorosilanes recovered from hydrogen recovery.
  3. Deposition: vaporised TCS mixed with H2 deposits Si on heated thin silicon rods (surface about 1,000 °C).
  4. Hydrogen recovery: reactor off-gas (unreacted TCS, H2, STC, HCl) is cooled so that chlorosilanes condense; H2 is purified and recycled.
  5. Finishing: cutting, crushing, etching, washing, drying, inspection, packing.
- System pressure about 0.1 MPa (gauge/absolute not stated).
- By-product STC is sent to a neighbouring company, which returns HCl from its own production.

**Major equipment**
- Hydrogen recovery train (unit HR-6): demister (removes solids) → water-cooled exchangers HX-A / HX-B / HX-C → gas-gas exchanger GG-1 → low-temperature exchanger LT-1 → condensate tanks → hydrogen purification (HCl separated and recovered, H2 purified).
- HX-A, HX-B, HX-C water-cooled exchangers (three lines; normally two running, one standby). Each: vertical shell-and-tube, installed upright; process gas in at the upper channel cover, down through the tubes, out at the lower channel cover via an L-shaped outlet pipe; cooling water in at the lower shell, out at the upper shell. Heat-transfer area 101 m² (tube ID basis); 293 tubes, OD 34 mm, ID 28.2 mm, length 4,000 mm; outer dimensions 912 mm diameter × 5,996 mm long; weight 4,800 kg empty, 6,300 kg operating. Shell and channel covers SUS304; tubes and tubesheets SUS316L. Upper channel cover about 300 kg, bolted at a flange, carries the gas inlet valve. Operating temperature and pressure not stated.
- Chlorosilane polymers (compounds with chains of two or more Si atoms, formed from SiCl2 produced in the deposition reactors) deposit on the tube inner walls of HX-A/B/C during operation, narrowing the flow path. Changeover to the standby exchanger is decided from outlet temperature and pressure drop, and a fixed service period has applied for the last 3 years. HX-B (now removed) is estimated to hold several hundred kg of deposits.
- Fluidised-bed chlorination furnace (about 300 °C).
- Distillation columns, including crude distillation column No. 2 with a pipe rack on its north side; separate DCS distillation unit and DCS production plant.
- Deposition reactors.
- Off-gas treatment: purge gas bubbled through water in a tank, then an NaOH gas scrubber, then to atmosphere.
- Wastewater: drainage pit, drainage tank facility and neutralisation.
- Activated carbon adsorption unit, next to the west side of the washing area (resin piping, slate wall, steel frame).
- Heat-exchanger washing area: open area enclosed with flame-retardant resin sheeting, temporary floodlights, temporary high-pressure jet-water tank and pump; timber supports for exchangers.

**Lines and connections**
- Exchanger changeover valves (A–G) on the three water-cooled lines: open the standby line valves, close the valves on the line being removed, discharge the process gas from the isolated line, then remove the exchanger; production continues on the standby line.
- Temporary dry-nitrogen hoses for purging removed exchangers.
- Temporary humidified-nitrogen supply: dry nitrogen bubbled through water in a closed vessel (about 0.8 vol% H2O).
- Purge exhaust from removed exchangers to the water tank and NaOH scrubber.

**Chemicals on site**
- Trichlorosilane, silicon tetrachloride, dichlorosilane (liquids/vapours; react with water).
- Hydrogen, hydrogen chloride.
- Chlorosilane polymers (solid/viscous deposits in exchangers).
- Sodium hydroxide solution (scrubber, neutralisation).
- Metallurgical silicon powder; nitrogen.

**Pre-opening treatment already carried out on HX-B** (per the current work standard)
1. Seven weeks ago: HX-B taken out of service; cooling water drained; process side purged with dry nitrogen.
2. Next day: disconnected and moved to the temporary storage area.
3. Over the following week, on three days: dry nitrogen blow at 20 Nm³/h and 0.018 MPa (gauge/absolute not stated) to remove residual chlorosilanes, HCl and H2.
4. Then 20 working days, about 09:00–17:00 each day: humidified nitrogen blow (about 0.8 vol% H2O) to hydrolyse the polymer surface so that less HCl and H2 is released at opening; no temperature control (ambient, winter conditions). Total water introduced about 11 kg.
5. Nine-day year-end holiday.
6. The last three days: dry nitrogen blow to remove HCl and H2 formed by hydrolysis.
- The work standard gives no quantitative end-point for the hydrolysis step. Work standards are approved by the section manager and may be changed within the section.

**Planned work** (today)
1. About 07:30: fill HX-B process side with dry nitrogen to a slight positive pressure; close valves.
2. About 09:00: move HX-B to the washing area with the gas inlet and outlet pointing upward; replace the channel-cover flange bolts beforehand (seized bolts delay opening).
3. Connect dry-nitrogen hoses to both channel covers; blow nitrogen at 20 Nm³/h and 0.018 MPa; draw exhaust from the underside of the lower cover to the NaOH scrubber.
4. With nitrogen blowing, remove the L-shaped outlet pipe; remove the lower channel cover with the crane truck; wash with normal- and high-pressure water; cover the opening with vinyl sheet.
5. Afternoon: remove the gas inlet valve from the upper channel cover; open the upper channel cover, hung from the crane truck; wash the tubes with high-pressure water.
- People: a production safety manager directing seven production staff; an equipment-department construction officer with three staff; a contractor site supervisor with six workers and one crane-truck operator. Other staff will attend around the exchanger to observe and for training.

**Instrumentation and safeguards**
- Protective equipment required for exchanger opening (polymers known to be able to ignite).
- Nitrogen blanketing during opening; scrubber on the exhaust.
- History: 3 years ago a burn injury during dismantling of an exchanger (corrective actions not followed up). 2 years ago an explosion in the drainage tank facility when NaOH contacted residue from dismantling and cleaning a gas-gas exchanger; attributed to fast hydrogen release from polymer plus alkali, and judged not possible without alkali.
- Hazard levels of non-routine work are not classified; training does not cover polymer hazards.

**Surroundings**
- Washing area is in the open within the plant, near the activated carbon unit and the No. 2 crude distillation pipe rack.
- Neighbouring companies (fumed-silica producer, oil company) in the same industrial complex.
