# Plant S: refinery sulfuric acid tank farm — catwalk repair

## Data package

**Process / products**
- Riverside crude-oil refinery, about 650 employees and 300 contractors on site; maintenance by a resident contractor. Joint union/management health and safety committee.
- Sulfuric acid alkylation unit combines isobutane and butylenes into alkylate. Fresh acid about 99% H2SO4 / 1% water. Spent acid 88–95% H2SO4, up to 5% water, remainder hydrocarbons (light hydrocarbons typically under 1%, including isobutane and C5–C16 isoparaffin alkylates, which float on the acid).
- On-site acid regeneration plant (decomposes spent acid to SO2 and water, converts SO2 to SO3, absorbs to make fresh acid); its capacity is not sufficient, so excess spent acid is shipped to an off-site contractor.

**Major equipment**
- Acid tank farm: six carbon-steel tanks TK-1 to TK-6, built about 22 years ago to API 650. Each 47 ft diameter × 32 ft high, nominal 415,000 gal; four 8-ft shell courses, plate 9/16 in (bottom), 7/16 in, 5/16 in (top two courses and roof). Total inventory about 1.7 million gal. No emergency venting or frangible roof on any tank.
- TK-1 (spent acid): converted from fresh acid about 4 years ago (MOC for flame arrester only). Internal inspection 8 years ago found severe horizontal corrosion rings about 8 ft above the floor (repaired).
- TK-2 (spent acid): converted from fresh acid last year.
- TK-3 (spent acid, currently about 264,000 gal): conversion from fresh to spent acid service about 15 months ago (switched between services several times since; spent service for the last 3 months) done by work order to the maintenance contractor: add a flame arrester and vacuum breaker, connect a CO2 hose. No MOC review, inerting calculations, HAZOP or pre-start-up review for the conversion. Fittings: conservation (pressure/vacuum) vent with flame arrester. CO2 inerting via a 3/4-in flexible rubber hose teed off TK-6's CO2 piping, run along the catwalk and dropped into a roughly 1.5-in hole in TK-3's roof where a corroded nozzle fell off (installed as temporary; in place more than a year). Level measured by a bubbler injecting instrument air near the floor. An overflow line connects TK-3's vapour space to TK-4 and TK-5. Insulation removed from a wide area of the west and southwest shell after leak repairs.
- TK-3 condition: 9 years ago, about half the corrosion allowance used over large areas; last internal inspection 7 years ago (270° corrosion groove 4 in above the floor, weld build-up repair; thickness readings recommended for 2 years later not taken). Shell leaks repaired with plates 3 years, 2 years and 2 years ago; horizontal grooving leaks 15 months ago (epoxy patch, then steel); a 3 × 1/8 in shell leak 2 months ago — the recommended 22 × 22 in patch not installed, liquid level lowered below the leak instead. Inspectors have recommended an internal inspection "as soon as possible" in each of the last three years; it has been deferred (now scheduled about 6 months from now) as part of a tank inspection reprioritisation that also deferred eight other tanks.
- TK-4 (fresh acid, vent open to atmosphere, not inerted): vertical corrosion at the level bubbler 9 years ago, repaired with 3–6 ft wide plates about 24 ft up.
- TK-5 (fresh acid, not inerted): corrosion allowance nearly used 9 years ago, limited to 25 ft of liquid; condemned after an internal inspection 6 years ago and partly replaced.
- TK-6 (spent acid, about 352,000 gal): hard-piped CO2 inerting; a 1.5-in nozzle has corroded off its roof, leaving an opening.
- CO2 regulator RG-1: one 1-in regulator serving both TK-6 and TK-3, rated 45,300 scfh CO2 at 80 psig inlet, set to 2 in water column; senses pressure in the downstream piping, not in the tanks. No pressure gauge on the TK-3 feed. The other spent-acid tanks have dedicated hard-piped regulators.
- Catwalk at roof level linking the tanks (access to gauge hatches, nozzles, instruments, inerting controls); about 25 ft long between TK-6 and TK-3; grating corroded by acid fumes; at TK-3 it runs over the southwest part of the roof.
- Common dike around the tank farm, 180 ft × 130 ft × 5 ft high, about 480,000 gal capacity; no individual tank dikes. Spill collection boxes drain to a common header, a valve box, an internal sump and an acid neutralisation system.
- Alkylation unit acid tanks TK-20 and TK-28 (TK-20 retired and replaced after an internal inspection 5 years ago; TK-28 found holed in many places 3 years ago and repaired with 8 × 8 ft plates).

**Lines and connections**
- Spent acid from the alkylation unit to TK-1, TK-2, TK-3, TK-6; fresh acid from regeneration to TK-4, TK-5; product withdrawal lines from each tank.
- CO2 supply → RG-1 → TK-6 hard piping, with tee → 3/4-in hose → TK-3 roof hole.
- TK-3 overflow line to TK-4 / TK-5 vapour spaces.
- Instrument air to level bubblers.
- Oily-water sewer to the wastewater treatment plant; stormwater sewer to a utility channel and the river.

**Chemicals on site**
- Fresh sulfuric acid (about 99%), TK-4, TK-5.
- Spent sulfuric acid (88–95%, with hydrocarbons), TK-1, TK-2, TK-3, TK-6. The refinery's MSDS for spent acid gives an NFPA fire rating of 0 and states the product is not combustible.
- Sulfur dioxide (acid fumes around the tanks and catwalk); carbon dioxide (inerting).
- Isobutane, butylenes, alkylate (alkylation unit).

**Procedures**
1. Hot work permits issued by operations from the area control room; gas test by an outside operator with portable meters (O2, LEL, H2S, SO2), co-signed by a unit supervisor, acknowledged by the contractor foreman. Site instructions: the gas test applies for the duration of the permit; fixed or personal monitors are recommended if conditions might change; in practice retests are done only if workers ask.
2. Site standard for air arcing outside vessels requires strict spark confinement ("boxing in").
3. Unsafe condition reports (UCR) go to area supervision, refinery safety, the joint safety committee and the control room, and are referred to a safety team that meets monthly; operators are not required to read UCRs.
4. Tank inspection under the refinery's tank inspection programme (prioritised list agreed this year, including tanks under a regulator's order).

**Planned work** (today)

2. Hot work permit for burning, welding and grinding on the catwalk at TK-6, valid 08:00–16:30. Requirements: fire watch, acid-gas respirators, warning signs, impervious gloves, face shields, coordination with operations, stop work if hydrocarbons are detected. No spark containment, periodic retest or continuous gas monitoring required. Cutting method not specified on the permit; the crew has an oxy-acetylene torch and air carbon arc gouging equipment (air at 80–100 psig).
3. Previous permit requests for this job were refused once for 10 ppm SO2 at the bottom of the stairs, and once (3 weeks ago) for a 1% combustible gas reading and the hole in TK-6's roof. After the second refusal, an operator filed a UCR describing the CO2 hose through TK-3's roof hole, the open nozzle on TK-6, and the single regulator for both tanks; a safety representative added that air masks are needed on the top walkway because of SO2 and acid fumes. The UCR arrived after the monthly safety team meeting and has not yet been discussed; no interim measures.
4. Weather: summer day, temperature rising from about 71 °F in the morning.

**Instrumentation and safeguards**
- Conservation vents and flame arresters on the spent-acid tanks; CO2 inerting at 2 in water column via RG-1.
- Portable gas meters for permits.
- Refinery fire brigade.
- A process hazard analysis exists for the tank farm. Tank farm is outside the process safety management rule (atmospheric storage).

**Surroundings**
- Area control room; alkylation unit; acid regeneration plant; wastewater treatment plant; fly-ash settling ponds; a creek tributary and the river.
