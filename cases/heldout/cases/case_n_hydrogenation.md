# Plant N: hydrogenation unit of a styrene monomer / propylene oxide plant

## Data package

**Process / products**
- Petrochemical site beside a river estuary making base chemicals (ethylene, propylene) in a lower-olefins cracker, plus two styrene monomer / propylene oxide (SM/PO) plants. More than 800 employees.
- SM/PO chemistry: ethylbenzene + oxygen gives ethylbenzene hydroperoxide; with propylene and a catalyst this gives propylene oxide, methylphenylcarbinol (MPC) and methylphenyl ketone (MPK). MPC is converted to styrene monomer. By-product MPK is converted back to MPC in a separate hydrogenation unit.
- Plant N2 (the newer SM/PO plant, about 15 years in service; jointly owned, the site operator holds the permit) contains hydrogenation unit U-H, the subject of this package. Three sister plants abroad use virtually the same process.
- U-H converts MPK to MPC with hydrogen over a catalyst. The reaction is exothermic; heat is removed by liquid ethylbenzene flowing over the catalyst.

**Major equipment**
- R-1 and R-2 trickle-bed reactors (open columns of catalyst pellets, gas and liquid flowing down together under gravity), of different diameters. Design temperature 210 °C. At the top of each, a distribution plate (high-dispersion tray) spreads ethylbenzene and nitrogen to wet the bed ("shower effect"). A limited number of temperature sensors in each reactor bed. Normal pressure difference across each catalyst bed 20–50 mbar.
- V-1 liquid/gas separation vessel for R-1; V-2 for R-2. Level settings: high-high trip 87%, high alarm 72%, low alarm 28%, low-low trip 13%. Pressure relief devices on both separators.
- H-1 combined heating/cooling installation for the circulating liquid.
- E-1 gas condensing unit.
- P-1 central circulation pump.
- Catalyst (both reactors): cylindrical pellets containing copper, chromium and barium (copper oxide and barium chromate). Replaced every few years as activity declines. Current product "Catalyst B" from a supplier selected about 14 years ago as a drop-in replacement for the original "Catalyst A" (no equipment or procedure changes). Catalyst A hexavalent chromium content 0.1–0.2 wt%. Catalyst B: about 3 years ago the manufacturer changed its manufacturing process; hexavalent chromium content up to 5 wt% (within the agreed specification; shown in the safety data sheet). Catalyst selection tests (about 15 years ago) were dry reductions with H2/N2 without ethylbenzene, focused on normal production.
- Plant N1 (older SM/PO plant, about 38 years in service): its hydrogenation uses a liquid-full reactor with Catalyst A.
- Styrene monomer section of plant N2.
- Lower-olefins cracker: cracks gasoil, naphtha and LPG to ethylene and propylene.
- Site fire station on the industrial estate.

**Lines and connections**
- Ethylbenzene circulation: P-1 → H-1 → distribution plates of R-1 and R-2 → V-1 / V-2 → P-1.
- Nitrogen supply to the R-1 and R-2 distribution plates; flow stops if reactor pressure exceeds the nitrogen supply pressure.
- Hydrogen supply to R-1 and R-2 (used in the reduction step and in production).
- Gas discharge line from the separators to the flare, with control valve PV-F. PV-F closes automatically on separator high-high level (87%) and must be reset manually by the panel operator before it reopens.
- E-1 gas condensing unit on the vent gas.

**Chemicals on site**
- Ethylbenzene (liquid, flammable), circulating in U-H and stored on site.
- Hydrogen (gas), supply to U-H.
- Nitrogen (gas), utility.
- MPK, MPC, propylene oxide, styrene monomer, propylene, ethylene (plant streams).
- Catalyst pellets (Cu/Cr/Ba), fresh charge in both reactors.

**Design basis documents (licensor design, about 18 years old)**
- Heating rate for start-up heating phase: 30 °C/h.
- Nitrogen flow for wetting via the distribution plates: 475 kg/h.
- Ethylbenzene circulation: about 88 t/h to R-1, about 22 t/h to R-2.
- Laboratory test (about 37 years ago, liquid-full reactor) found no reaction between ethylbenzene and catalyst; ethylbenzene is treated as inert in the unit's safety studies. Wetting/runaway risk was identified for the reduction and production phases; no pressure relief is sized for a runaway.
- Safety report scenarios for plant N2 (10 scenarios) include none for U-H. Reactive hazard assessment (3–4 years ago) focused on the styrene monomer section.

**Procedures** (job analysis written by experienced panel operators for start-up after catalyst change)
1. Release oxygen, then leak test.
2. Flush with ethylbenzene to remove contamination.
3. Fill with clean ethylbenzene and circulate (circulation phase).
4. Heat up (heating phase).
5. Reduce the catalyst with hydrogen; only at the correct temperature and after at least 6 h of hot ethylbenzene circulation.
- The work instructions do not state a heating rate or a nitrogen flow. The control system is configured for normal production; there are no automated control loops for heating; start-up is under manual control. Separator level control is in manual mode during start-up.
- Operating philosophy: Stabilise, Slow down, Stop when limits are exceeded; during start-up the duty to intervene may be waived only if this does not create an unsafe situation.

**Instrumentation and safeguards**
- Reactor bed temperature indications (limited number per bed) and temperature alarms.
- Differential pressure across each reactor bed.
- Separator level alarms and trips as above (high-high closes PV-F).
- Pressure alarm in the gas discharge system (12 bar; gauge/absolute not stated).
- Pressure relief devices on V-1 and V-2.
- Extra temperature safeguards added after a start-up about 15 years ago, when hydrogen introduced too fast and in excess caused a temperature excursion to about 200 °C.
- Sister-plant history (4 years ago, same unit type, Catalyst B): temperature excursion during the heating phase to more than 685 °C with the flare discharge closed; attributed by the company to a leaking hydrogen valve.
