# Plant M: refinery light-ends (saturated gas) fractionation section

## Data package

**Process / products**
- Coastal crude-oil refinery, maximum throughput about 225,000 US bbl/d (11.4 Mt/yr); products include petroleum coke, LPG, gasoline, diesel and aviation fuel. Site area 480 acres, split by a dual carriageway. Top-tier major-hazard site; public information zone 1 km.
- The saturated gas fractionation section (in service about 20 years) separates combined light-ends feeds into fuel gas, propane and butane in three columns in series: de-ethaniser C-1, stabiliser C-2, propane/butane splitter C-3.
- Upstream and downstream connections link the section with the fluid catalytic cracker (FCC) and a cryogenic gas unit.

**Major equipment**
- D-1 feed surge drum; combined feeds arrive through feed line L-FEED and D-1 feeds C-1.
- C-1 de-ethaniser; removes mainly methane, ethane and propane overhead.
- C-2 stabiliser.
- C-3 propane/butane splitter.
- E-1A / E-1B water-cooled overhead condensers on C-1; partly condense the overhead (mainly to propane); uncondensed vapour goes to refinery fuel gas. Shells and tube baffles of both found badly corroded at an inspection 7 years ago (attributed to inadequate water wash and draw) and both replaced the same year.
- D-2 de-ethaniser reflux drum; sour-water draw and sampling point.
- L-OVHD C-1 overhead line, 6 in nominal, carbon steel, original wall thickness about 7–8 mm; runs from the top of C-1 to E-1A/B. Carries high-pressure gas of about 90% ethane/propane/butane with hydrogen sulphide.
- Cryogenic gas unit, connected to the FCC by a high-pressure gas line fitted with slam-shut valves.
- Closed blowdown unit.
- Aromatics unit with hydrogen compressor.
- Coker drill-water tank (contaminated water from the cokers).
- Windowless central control room with TV monitors of the plant.

**Lines and connections**
- L-FEED: combined light-ends feeds to D-1.
- WI-1: wash-water injection into L-FEED ahead of D-1, into liquid. Part of the original design; used continuously to remove corrosive agents from the feed. There was a previous failure at this injection location.
- WI-2: wash-water injection into L-OVHD. Added shortly after the section was commissioned, to control fouling of E-1A/B by salts and hydrates; installed by piping water into an existing 1-inch vent connection on L-OVHD, 670 mm upstream of a 90° bend, about 9 m above ground. No injection quill or dispersion device. Installed without a technical memorandum or multidisciplinary review. Use history: continuous for about 14 years; then intermittent for about 5 years; flow orifice size changed under a management-of-change form about 14 months ago (the change reduced the flow; the form showed the records of the original orifice size were inaccurate); returned to continuous use in the last few months without a management-of-change form. Staff outside plant operations generally understand WI-2 to be used only occasionally or to be out of service.
- C-1 overhead vapour from E-1A/B to fuel gas system.
- Cryogenic unit / FCC high-pressure gas line with slam-shut valves and manual isolation valves.
- Gasoline rundown line with manual valve.

**Chemicals on site**
- Light hydrocarbons (methane, ethane, propane, butane) with hydrogen sulphide, in the fractionation section.
- Sour water (from D-1 and D-2 draws).
- Crude oil, intermediates and products across the refinery; petroleum coke.

**Procedures**
1. Operating manual (revised about 6 months ago): WI-2 is to be commissioned at start-up. The manual does not cover shutdown or intermittent use of WI-2.
2. Switching between running and standby of water injection is done by operations.
3. Sour-water sampling at D-1 and D-2 for corrosion monitoring: D-2 samples are recorded as the best indicator of corrosion in the C-1 overhead; no D-2 results recorded in the last 4 years; D-1 sampling stopped for 3 years and resumed last year.
4. Corrosion probes at the D-1 outlet and the E-1A inlet (installed 7 years ago); the E-1A probe has shown a high corrosion rate.

**Inspection and integrity**
- In-house inspection group, independent of production, with a quality system.
- Written scheme of examination (WSE) for the section, last revised 4 years ago: generic pressure-piping inspection interval 60 months or less. L-OVHD has no specific WSE requirement. Water injection points are not listed in the WSE or in the piping inspection procedures.
- Piping inspection database (replaced 4 years ago) is poorly populated; inspection backlog about 70 staff-weeks last year. Equipment records migrated to a new maintenance system this year.
- L-OVHD was last thickness-checked 7 years ago, at the WI-2 injection point (pitting found; frequent inspection recommended). Readings planned 5 years ago were not taken because the scaffolding had been removed. The bend downstream of WI-2 has no recorded inspection in about 20 years.
- WI-1 area: inspected after the earlier failure at its injection location.
- Risk-based inspection (RBI) study of the section completed about 5 months ago, the first to include pipework. Software inputs: material, fluid, temperature, pressure (no inspection history). WI-2 was excluded because the RBI coordinator believed the line permanently isolated. L-OVHD was rated high risk; no acceptance criteria defined; next inspection planned in about 3 months.
- A company technical bulletin (9 years ago) warned that carbon steel next to water injection points is vulnerable; the corrosion engineer's follow-up review covered water-wash systems in continuous use. The full-time corrosion engineer has spent about 75% of his time on other projects for the last 4 years.

**Planned work**
1. None in this section. Normal operation; a public holiday, so about 185 people are on site (about 800 on a normal weekday). Shift handover at 15:00.

**Instrumentation and safeguards**
- Control valves on the section operable from the central control room (can be switched to manual and closed).
- Refinery general alarm; significant incident plan; on-site fire service with mutual aid from the neighbouring refinery.
- Slam-shut valves on the FCC–cryogenic unit high-pressure gas line.
- Off-site community alarm, reserved for foreseen toxic releases.

**Surroundings**
- Village of about 1,100 people about 0.5 km west; town of about 11,000 people about 1.5 km northwest; another village of about 300 people about 1.5 km northwest.
- Neighbouring refinery to the north across a railway line.
- Occupied buildings within 400 m of the section.
